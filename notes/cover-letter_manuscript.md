# Cover letter: Northeast LSOG manuscript

Dear Editor,

Please consider the enclosed manuscript, "It depends how you count: definition, disturbance history, and the distribution of late-successional and old-growth forest across the northeastern United States," for publication.

As wall-to-wall maps of mature and old-growth forest, prompted in part by the 2022 federal executive order, begin to anchor parcel-level conservation spending, two questions become decision-relevant: how much late-successional and old-growth (LSOG) forest exists, and how much do independent, equally defensible classifications agree on where it is. We address both for the Northeast. Using the Forest Inventory and Analysis probability sample, we provide design-based older-forest estimates with confidence intervals; we develop a four-axis LSOG classification (live structure, dead wood, composition, and temporal continuity measured from the full Landsat disturbance record) and quantify how the qualifying share depends on how many axes a definition requires; and we conduct a full cross-map assessment over Maine's unorganized townships, comparing three remote-sensing classifications that differ in sensor, training data, and target definition.

The central results are that true LSOG (all four axes) is far rarer than any single axis implies, ranging from about 3 percent in Maine to 12 to 15 percent in its neighbors; that Maine's low share reflects its production role in a regional division of forest function rather than a deficit to be closed state by state; that the older-forest stock is stable to rising even as mapped LSOG is harvested, because younger stands age into the older condition faster than older stands are removed; and that independent maps disagree by 1.6 to 2.6 fold in extent and agree on only a small fraction of the specific ground, so the choice of map drives most of the spending it informs. We recommend that regional LSOG accounting report a method range with design-based intervals, that the combined late-successional plus old-growth class be the stable policy unit, and that maps be cross-checked and field-verified before they steer acquisition.

A short companion Comment in Ecosphere presents the focused critique of a single published map; this manuscript is the constructive companion that builds the multi-axis classification and the full assessment. All code and derived products are openly archived at Zenodo (concept DOI 10.5281/zenodo.20614496); no Forest Inventory and Analysis plot coordinates are released. The work has not been published previously and is not under consideration elsewhere.

Thank you for your consideration.

Sincerely,
