# Simulated peer review v4 - near-submission, Ecosphere format and length compliance

Context: this round simulates an Ecosphere editorial screen plus three reviewers on the current draft (~8,160 words, 8 figures, 8 tables, single-spaced 16-page typeset PDF). The dominant issue this round is article-class compliance: Ecosphere Comments are capped at 16 *manuscript* pages, double-spaced (text, literature cited, tables, and figure legends all double-spaced; figures as separate files). The current content is roughly double the allowed length.

---

## Editor-in-Chief desk screen (compliance and scope)

Decision orientation: as submitted, this would be returned at desk screen for length before review.

1. Length. At ~8,160 words plus 8 tables and 8 figure captions, the double-spaced manuscript is ~28 to 32 pages against a 16-page cap. This must be cut by roughly half. The cut is the first task; everything else is secondary.
2. Display items. Eight figures and eight tables is far beyond Comment norms (typically 1 to 3 total). Most must move to Appendices (Ecological Archives) or be deleted.
3. Format. Convert to double-spaced, 12-pt Times, 1-inch margins, figures as separate files, tables after Literature Cited, figure captions listed separately, no abstract. Title should track the original up to any colon, then ": Comment."
4. Scope creep. The draft spans cross-map disagreement, dead-wood blindness, a design-based trend, a UT clip, regional context, protection, an ensemble prototype, a four-axis classification, drivers, continuity, carbon, and a synthesis table. That is two or three papers. Pick the single most decisive claim (credible maps disagree and the quantities lack intervals, so a single map should not drive parcel-level spending) and support it with the minimum necessary evidence. Move the constructive classification and ensemble to a separate methods paper or the appendices.

## Reviewer 1 (remote sensing / LiDAR)

General: The reproduction (94.2 vs 94.1) and the cross-map disagreement are the strongest, most defensible contributions and should anchor a shortened Comment. The dead-wood R-squared result (canopy blind to CWD) is excellent and central. Keep these.

Specific:
- The new HCS harvest-probability drivers result is interesting but the weakest-supported new claim: the harvest model and the LSOG classifier both key on mature structure, so the positive association is partly mechanical. Present it briefly with that caveat explicit, or move it to an appendix. Do not let it headline.
- TreeMap and canopy-height "maps" are proxies you built; at half the length, do not overclaim three independent maps. The Pelz external check is the cleaner independence argument; keep that, compress the constructed-map detail.
- Geolocation/co-registration caveat is good and can be one sentence.

## Reviewer 2 (forest biometrics)

General: The design-based FIA baseline with intervals is the right backbone and the UT-clip answer to the footprint question is convincing. Preserve this in full.

Specific:
- Keep: design-based older-forest estimate with CI; the rising trend with the gross-flux-vs-net-stock distinction; the UT-AOI clip (one sentence in text; Table to appendix).
- The four-axis classification (6.5%) is a genuine contribution but is really a second paper. In a Comment, compress to two sentences and a pointer, or cut. Funnel figure and Table 7 to an appendix.
- Carbon-by-class is a nice stakes line but tangential to a Comment about map fitness; cut or one clause.

## Reviewer 3 (ecologist; original-author perspective)

General: The tone is now genuinely constructive and credits our Limitations and future-work proposals. At length, the Comment risks reading as a rival synthesis rather than a comment on our paper. Shorten and it lands better.

Specific:
- The pre-emptive-harvest claim ("landowners accelerating harvest ahead of regulation") is anecdotal and unreferenced. Either attribute it to a citable source or frame it explicitly as a hypothesis/risk, not an observed effect. Soften and label it anecdotal.
- The breadth-is-objective-dependent framing is fair and welcome.
- The slope-vs-harvest "two-sided" framing is good but is a paragraph, not a section.

## Editor synthesis

Recommendation: Major revision, contingent on length. Required:
1. Cut to <= 16 double-spaced manuscript pages (target ~3,800 to 4,200 words of body text).
2. Reduce to 2 to 3 main-text figures and 2 to 3 main-text tables; move the rest to Appendices. Suggested main-text keep: the cross-map disagreement figure and the design-based trend (figure or table); one summary table.
3. Soften/attribute the pre-emptive-harvest claim.
4. Reformat to ESA manuscript style (double-spaced, figures separate, tables at end, no abstract).
5. Preserve the core thesis and strongest evidence (reproduction, cross-map disagreement, dead-wood blindness, design-based baseline with intervals, UT clip). Demote classification, ensemble, regional, protection, continuity, carbon, and drivers to brief mentions plus appendices.

The underlying science is solid and well-verified; the task is editorial discipline, not new analysis.
