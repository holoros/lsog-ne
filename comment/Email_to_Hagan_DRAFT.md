**Subject:** Quick note on your Ecosphere LSOG paper

John,

I've spent real time with your Ecosphere LSOG paper and the archived data, and first I want to say how much I appreciate the work, and especially that you made the training data and code public. I reproduced your random forest cleanly (94.2% against your 94.1%), and the field effort behind it is obvious.

I'm reaching out because the map is starting to drive some big decisions. The Thompson/PERC piece prices the top half of the patches at $200-300 million for LD 1529, and that raised questions I wanted to put in front of you directly rather than only in a venue. Using your own training plots and some independent public data over the same area, I looked at how the classification holds up as a basis for that kind of allocation: how well the canopy metrics capture the deadwood and large-tree structure that define old growth, and how much the map agrees with independent operationalizations of LSOG at the landscape scale. The short version is that training accuracy is high for every approach, but independent maps disagree by roughly 2.8x on amount and on most of the location, which matters a lot when specific hectares get prioritized.

I've drafted this up as a Comment (attached). I am genuinely undecided about whether to submit it, and I would rather you see it first. My real hope is that it is useful input for a follow-up analysis, a cross-map accuracy assessment and an uncertainty statement, before the current map gets locked into policy and markets. I think that would make the underlying contribution stronger, not weaker.

Happy to talk any time, and I am glad to share all the code and data behind what I did.

Thanks again,
Aaron
