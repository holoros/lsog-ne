# Draft email to John Hagan (jhagan@ourclimatecommon.org)

**To:** jhagan@ourclimatecommon.org
**Subject:** Your Ecosphere LSOG paper — a few comparisons worth a look

John,

Good to see the LSOG work come out, and congratulations on it. I spent real time with the paper and your archived data, partly because the CFRU connection made me want to understand it well, and the first thing I want to say is that the transparency is exactly right. I reproduced your random forest cleanly (94.2% against your 94.1%), and the field effort behind it is obvious.

I am writing because the map is starting to carry a lot of weight. The Thompson and Daigneault report prices the top half of the patches at $200-300 million for LD 1529, and that level of use raised questions I would rather put to you directly than only in a venue. Using your own training plots and some independent public data over the same area, I looked at how the classification holds up as a basis for that kind of allocation: how well the canopy metrics capture the deadwood and large-tree structure that define old growth, how much the map agrees with independent operationalizations of LSOG, and what the FIA design-based ground estimates say once you put confidence intervals on them. The short version is that training accuracy is high for every approach, but independent maps disagree by roughly 2.8x on amount and on most of the location, and the FIA ground record puts older forest at about 3.9% with a 3.3 to 4.6% interval and a slightly rising trend rather than a rapid decline.

I drafted this as a Comment (attached, with a short supplement). I am honestly undecided about submitting it, and I would rather you see it first. My real hope is that it is useful input for a follow-up, a cross-map accuracy assessment and an uncertainty statement, before the current map gets locked into policy and markets. I think that makes the underlying contribution stronger, not weaker.

Happy to talk any time, and all the code and data behind what I did are openly archived.

Thanks again,
Aaron
