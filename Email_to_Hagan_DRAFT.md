# Draft email to John Hagan (jhagan@ourclimatecommon.org)

**To:** jhagan@ourclimatecommon.org
**Subject:** July 8, and a Comment that turned constructive, I want you to see it first

John,

Congratulations again on the LSOG paper. Regina mentioned the July 8 field day and I would like to join if there is room; getting out with you and your team to see the stands is the right way to ground all of this. I have spent real time with your paper and your archived data, partly because of the CFRU connection, and I want to be clear up front that I read the work as careful and transparent. You are candid about the pitfalls, including that airborne LiDAR cannot cleanly separate late-successional from old-growth structure, the data and code are open, and I reproduced your random forest cleanly (94.2% against your 94.1%). I also understand the MNAP truthing shows the model under-predicts in places, which fits the larger point rather than undercutting it.

What started as a critique has become, I think, a genuinely constructive piece, and that is the version I want you to have. Briefly, it does four things: it quantifies the cross-map and ground uncertainty your analysis did not report; it treats FIA as the unbiased baseline for how much older forest exists, while being explicit that FIA is imprecise at its plot density and that your wall-to-wall layer may catch small patches the plots miss, so the two are complementary, not rival; it shows that LSOG is genuinely multidimensional (live structure, dead wood, composition, and temporal continuity do not collapse to one axis); and it proposes a repeatable, FIA-anchored, four-axis classification of true LSOG with an explicit path to mapping each axis, including a first Landsat continuity layer. It also shows that LSOG concentrates where harvest is hard and disturbance is likely, which I think reframes the risk conversation in a useful way.

Jereme Frank has offered to put together a simple side-by-side of how much LSOG each method estimates statewide and by region, with the four axes spelled out, and to see whether CFRU can give me access to look under the hood of your layer. I would welcome that; you should be able to see exactly what I did, and I would rather build this with you than around you.

So, two tracks, and they are not in conflict. The first, my preference, is a grounded follow-up we do together: a cross-map accuracy assessment, the design-based baseline, the multi-axis classification, and the harvest-probability and Landsat continuity layers I am working on now. The second is that I submit the attached as a formal Comment to Ecosphere, where you would be invited to reply. Either way you see it first.

Let me know about July 8 and the data handoff, and I will coordinate with Regina and Jereme. Looking forward to it.

Aaron
