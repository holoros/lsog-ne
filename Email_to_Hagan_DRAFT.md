# Draft email to John Hagan (jhagan@ourclimatecommon.org)

**To:** jhagan@ourclimatecommon.org
**Subject:** July 8, and a formal Comment I want you to see first

John,

Congratulations on the LSOG paper. Regina mentioned you are heading into the field on July 8, and I would like to join if there is room. I have been spending real time with your paper and your archived data, partly because of the CFRU connection, and getting out with you and your team to see the stands is exactly the right way to ground all of it.

First, I want to be clear that I read your work as careful and transparent. You are candid about the pitfalls, including that airborne LiDAR cannot cleanly separate late-successional from old-growth structure, and the data and code are all in the open, which is how it should be. I reproduced your random forest cleanly (94.2% against your 94.1%), and nothing I have done questions the competence or the openness of the analysis. I also understand the MNAP truthing is showing the model under-predicts in places like western Maine, which fits the larger point: a single map carries real, locally variable error in both directions, and that is hard to avoid with this signal.

What I have put together is a cross-map comparison and an FIA design-based analysis over the same area, with confidence intervals on the quantities the policy conversation is now leaning on. The short version is that training accuracy is high for every approach, independent maps still disagree by roughly 2.8x on the broad LSOG class, and the unbiased FIA estimate puts older forest at about 3.9% (3.3 to 4.6) and rising, including on private commercial timberland. None of that overturns your science; it quantifies the uncertainty and puts error bars where there currently are none. Ian Prior at Seven Islands has worked on it with me.

I will be direct about where this is going, because I would rather you hear it from me than be surprised. I intend to submit this as a formal Comment to Ecosphere. The map is now steering real decisions, the Thompson and Daigneault cost estimate and the certification and policy questions you have been raising with the Maine Forest Products Council, and I do not think the published map should stand as the last word on something at that scale without the uncertainty quantified and the map checked against independent data. Your own Limitations section flags much of this, the weak old-growth classification, the inability to estimate ingrowth, how much definitions matter, and you recommend ground-truthing before decisions; what I have done is put numbers and confidence intervals on those caveats. Ecosphere invites the original authors to reply alongside a Comment, so you would have the floor to respond, and I wanted you to see the full draft before any of that rather than after. It is attached.

Separately and genuinely, the better long-term outcome is a grounded follow-up we do together: a proper cross-map accuracy assessment with field truthing and confidence intervals, built on your plots and the inventory. I would very much like to do that with you, and I do not see it as in conflict with the Comment. One puts the uncertainty on the record now; the other gets the science right for good.

Let me know about July 8 and the data handoff, and I will coordinate with Regina. Looking forward to it.

Aaron
