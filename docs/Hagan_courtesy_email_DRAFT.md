**Subject:** Follow-up on the Ecosphere LSOG map — analysis and a heads-up before we submit a Comment

John,

I wanted to follow up on our earlier exchange and give you a direct heads-up, in the spirit of professional courtesy, before anything goes to a journal.

Since we last talked, my group has worked through the LSOG mapping in some detail, using your archived training data together with independent public data (FIA, the USFS LCMS disturbance record, the ORNL mature-and-old-growth product) and a few field references we hold (the Maine ecological reserve network, including Big Reed, and the Baxter CFI). We reproduced your random forest closely (out-of-bag accuracy 94.2% against your 94.1%, same top predictor), so none of what follows questions the care or the openness of the original work, which I have said publicly and will say in print.

Our concern is narrower and methodological, and it has grown more specific as the map has become the basis for parcel-level conservation prioritization at the scale of a few hundred million dollars. In brief, we find that: independent, equally defensible maps of the same forest disagree substantially on where LSOG is; the reported area is a single number without a sampling interval and shifts a lot under routine modeling choices (class balancing roughly doubles the mapped old-growth area); the training data are a purposive rather than a probability sample, which leaves the rare old-growth class detected well under a third of the time and, in the field validation, not sampled at all; and the design-based FIA estimate, with its confidence interval, suggests the older-forest stock is stable to rising rather than rapidly declining once the harvest flux and the net stock are distinguished. We also build a constructive alternative — a multi-axis, FIA-anchored, uncertainty-quantified product — rather than only critiquing.

I am planning to submit a formal Comment to Ecosphere, with a longer companion analysis elsewhere, but I would genuinely rather you see the analysis first and have the chance to respond, correct anything I have wrong, or tell me where you see it differently. I am happy to share the full write-up, the figures, and the archived code and derived products (everything is openly deposited), and to talk by phone or in person if that is easier. If there are points where I have misread your methods or data, I want to fix them before submission, not after.

Could you let me know in the next couple of weeks whether you would like to see the materials and discuss? If I do not hear back, I will take that as a sign you would prefer to engage through the formal Comment process, which is of course entirely your call.

With respect and continued appreciation for the work,

Aaron
