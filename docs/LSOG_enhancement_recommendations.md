# Enhancing the LSOG analysis: recommendations from three source documents

Prepared 13 June 2026. Synthesis of (1) the RAP v2.0 field protocol (Shamgochian, Hagan, Taylor & Reed 2025, Our Climate Common), (2) the PERC prioritization report (Thompson, Daigneault, Plisinski, Moon & Norton 2026), and (3) the federal mature-and-old-growth threat analysis (USDA & USDI 2024, FS-1215c). Each is now cited correctly in the manuscript. Recommendations are ordered by leverage.

## What the three documents establish

RAP v2.0 is the field instrument behind the LiDAR map, and it is itself a 17-metric random forest, not a score. Two facts in it are decisive for our argument. First, dead wood is nearly inert in their classifier: large standing dead trees rank last of seventeen metrics (mean decrease in accuracy -0.3%) and large downed logs thirteenth (5.1%), even though their own text calls large logs "one of the best indicators of LSOG." Second, harvest-history evidence carries the model: sawn-stump decay stage (15.0%) and skid trails (13.0%) are the third and fourth most important metrics, behind only treefall gaps (17.1%) and large live trees (16.3%). The protocol was trained on 78 plots that were "mostly hardwood, with no purely softwood hectares," and its own field accuracy is 74% out-of-bag, not the 90% that belongs to the LiDAR map. The reference structural values are in their Appendix A (late-successional class: about 90 large trees per hectare, coarse woody material near 80 m^3/ha, 56% of basal area in trees at least 40 cm).

The PERC report builds the entire $422 million prioritization (about $200-300 million for the top half of patches, $3,799/ha average) directly and only on the single Hagan map, with no uncertainty carried forward. It delineates 22,429 late-successional-plus-old-growth patches by eight-neighbor connectivity on the binary raster, scores each on four equally weighted criteria (patch size, carbon, connectivity, modeled harvest threat), and ranks them. The report itself concedes that every metric "would need to be validated in the field before any conservation investment is committed," and flags that more than 75% of old-growth area sits in patches under 5 ha. The headline loss figure (about 15,000 ha since the 2016-2018 LiDAR flights) is attributed to Hagan et al. 2025, not computed there, and no annual loss rate appears in the report.

The federal threat analysis is the most useful external anchor. It states plainly that eastern old growth could not be evaluated in the national analysis "because there were few or no FIA plots," which is exactly the gap our state-targeted design-based estimate fills. Nationally, tree cutting is a small net threat (a 0.03% net old-growth decline on public land since 2000) but was historically the dominant cause of eastern old-growth loss, and the Eastern Region shows "no detectable" increase in slow or chronic disturbance. The federal mature-and-old-growth definition covers three of our four axes (large-tree structure, dead wood, composition) but has no explicit temporal-continuity axis, relying on old trees as a structural surrogate.

## Recommendations

1. Already folded in. The manuscript now (a) cites the RAP dead-wood and harvest-history importance ranking in the dead-wood-blind-spot section, turning a general claim into a specific, sourced one; (b) cites the federal "few or no FIA plots" statement to justify design-based estimation where the resource is rare; and (c) anchors the "tighter than RAP reference values" claim to the Appendix A class means. The Thompson, Shamgochian (RAP), and both federal reports are in the reference list.

2. Highest-leverage next step, and the natural home for the multi-model ensemble now running. Overlay the ensemble uncertainty surface on the PERC patch framework and show prioritization instability directly: what fraction of their top-ranked patches are robustly LSOG across all five models versus flagged by a minority, and how many top-50 patches change rank or fragment when patch boundaries are redrawn under model disagreement. Because patch size is one of four equally weighted criteria and most patches are tiny, boundary uncertainty propagates straight into the ranking. This converts our probabilistic map from a methods contribution into a direct policy demonstration: a buyer following the single-map ranking at $3,799/ha may pay for ground the ensemble does not confirm.

3. Restate the PERC cost as a range, not a point. Their $422 million assumes a fixed 161,881 ha of late-successional-plus-old-growth. Propagating our design-based area interval turns the headline into a band (roughly plus or minus a third), and the carbon-revenue offset (their $72-312 million, used to argue feasibility) shrinks under the same uncertainty because misclassified pixels carry less above-baseline carbon. One paragraph in the Comment's implications section, using their own numbers, makes the spending fragility quantitative.

4. Add a light threat or continuity-stability layer to the ensemble, using the federal framework's public inputs (LCMS slow/chronic disturbance, which we already process; road density; insect/disease hazard). The Eastern Region's "no detectable" chronic-disturbance signal becomes a conservation argument: candidate LSOG sits in a low-abiotic-disturbance region, so the threat that matters is harvest, which is exactly the flux-versus-stock distinction. This is a modest add that strengthens both the manuscript's working-landscape framing and the Comment's loss-premise rebuttal.

5. Build a RAP crosswalk as a short validation. RAP gives explicit age bands from increment cores (transitional 100-150 yr, late-successional 150-200 yr, old growth no harvest evidence). Mapping our FIA conditions onto RAP's four classes, and reporting how our LSOG calls distribute across them, is a concrete, citable bridge between the two definitions and pre-empts the "your FIA definition is not their LSOG" objection.

6. Correct the attribution precisely in the Comment. The 90% accuracy is the 2024 LiDAR map; RAP's field accuracy is 74%; the RAP authors are Shamgochian et al., not Hagan alone. State each cleanly so the critique cannot be deflected on a misattribution.
